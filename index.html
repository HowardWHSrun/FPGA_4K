Blockers and major items

  • SYS-54Option B cannot carry pin map V6: J5 would need at least 132 contacts, the DF40 series stops at 120, and the 5.3 mm router/LDO-board overlap has no room for a second connector. New
  • SYS-55If the Frontend is unplugged between sessions, as the records assume for Option A, FPGA-LDO A's two DF40C-70DP plugs sit bare on the head with 120 signal contacts wired straight to ASIC die pads and no ESD protection. New
  • SYS-56If all eight stacks share one head ground electrode, as SYS-11 advises, every J1 lead becomes a supply-return path through ASIC AGND (where REF_1-4 are bonded) and NT1, carrying about 4-27 mA in a typical lead and 9-62 mA (up to 89 mA) in the most-loaded one, so load steps put up to about 13 mV on the recording reference. New
  • SYS-57In Option A the Frontend's south edge, where its order asks for break-off tabs, sits only 0.567 mm from the near ASIC board beside it. New
  • SYS-58Enclosure V3 has no recorded room for the XEM8310's fansink, so the 10-15 W module has no heat sink in a box without a fan and may exceed its 0-70 C rating. New
  • R2S-03ASIC boards V7 are now 0.8 mm thick, so the head stack is back at about 13.8 mm, but the fabricator has not confirmed ten layers in 0.8 mm and the fit models were not rebuilt.
  • R2S-05Each port's 44 chip outputs return through two DGND contacts; the far chips' lines now run beside the DGND plane but still start over AGND.
  • SYS-01Each cable's one GT lane must carry eight ASICs, but the only recorded line rate (1.25 Gb/s) counts one ASIC.
  • SYS-02High-load stacks reach the 637 mA port current limit on a 1.8 m cable with 30-32 AWG or thinner +5 V wire.
  • SYS-03No record estimates or limits head-stack heat; about 1.45 W per stack gives a 19-34 K still-air surface rise.
  • SYS-06Option B J5 carries 108 signals with only three return contacts: GND on pins 3 and 65 and AGND on pin 62.
  • SYS-07No head board has a fixing feature, so the stack and the HDMI cable load hang on unlocked DF40 connectors.
  • SYS-09Unkeyed DF40 pairs let an ASIC board mate rotated 180 degrees, putting +/-8 V onto ASIC clock and data pins.
  • SYS-31No file records the height of the ASIC dies and bond loops, which sit 1.5 mm under the next ASIC board.

New in V3, smaller items

  • SYS-60STIM_EN, STIM_START, STIM_CLK and STIM_CHB reach all eight ASICs of a stack and the ASIC configuration cannot be read back, so an ASIC not loaded with a safe table can stimulate whenever another ASIC does. New
  • SYS-61No bring-up or handling procedure exists, yet 21 open items, four of them major, rely on one as their only protection for the first build. New
  • SYS-66Rocking either V4 ASIC board on or off with the cable live leaves +10 V or -10 V and three 1.5 V rails connected after all four ground contacts have opened. New
  • SYS-67One open feedback resistor (R700) lets the HDMI board's shared 5 V rail run up to about 12 V, with no overvoltage stop or clamp. New
  • SYS-68On the Frontend cable connector, the 5 V pad sits 0.15 mm from the FPGA's TCK pad, and neither JTAG line has a series resistor. New
  • SYS-70Neither the Frontend nor the FPGA-LDO A order asks for the 0.02 mm board flatness Hirose requires under each DF40, although both boards carry unbalanced copper. New
  • SYS-71The FPGA-LDO A order leaves break-off tab positions to JLC, although J1/J2 sit 0.88 mm from both long edges and only short edge stretches meet JLC's own tab keep-out. New
  • SYS-72In Option A the FPGA-LDO A connectors J1/J2 stay open on the animal's head whenever the Frontend is unplugged, with no cover and no cleaning method. New
  • SYS-74Even with 20 full ground stacks around J2, 11 west-side J2 lanes keep a 1.06-1.50 mm return path, so the 1.0 mm aim is not reachable there. New
  • SYS-64Under pin map V6 as traced, ASIC3 and ASIC7 each bring READ, DATA7 and DATA8 into a different FPGA bank from their returned clock, breaking the Frontend's one-bank-per-ASIC rule. New
  • SYS-75V6 puts the far chips' READ strobes on J3/J4 pin 2, 8.0 mm from the nearest ground contact, the worst position on the 80-pin joint. New
  • SYS-76The relayed start-up order loads SPI before CLKH starts, but the only recorded working firmware ran CLKH continuously and clocked its SPI engine from the returned clock, which exists only while CLKH runs. New
  • SYS-78The animal's reference electrode is tied to the host PC and 12 V adapter grounds, with no isolation and no leakage rule on record. New
  • SYS-79A bench supply hot-connected to the Frontend's TP14 can ring VIN_5V to 7.5-8 V, while cable hot-plug stays below about 5.6 V. New
  • SYS-80The ±12 V soft-start keeps the FPGA-LDO start-up surge near 0.2-0.37 A at 3.3 V, so a stack draws about 0.27-0.48 A at the 5 V pin during start-up, not the port limit that FE-02 states. New
  • SYS-81The ASIC designer's DVDD figure allows only about 5 pF per output, so the real output-driver current makes DVDD about 1.7-3.5 times the 1.39 mA per board in the budget. New
  • SYS-83Ground stacks fit within 1.1 mm of all eight FE-21 capacitors, but they take about 2.5% of MGTAVCC and 1.5% of MGTAVTT on L4. New

Closed

  • R2S-07No ASIC's DISC_OUT reaches the FPGA, yet the V3 question to the ASIC designer asks only about the top chips.
  • SYS-53On FPGA-LDO A V12 at most about 3-4 of the 12 new V6 lines get short routes through the existing copper; the rest need long multi-via detours, more L2 AGND cuts or moved lanes, so V6 needs a re-planned lane field, not 12 extra routes.
  • SYS-59The only complete V6 grid route found for FPGA-LDO A fails KiCad DRC, has 37 laser vias too close to same-net buried vias for JLC and cuts L2 AGND by 62 mm², so a re-route under V12's rules is still unproven.
  • SYS-63After V6 only 3 of the 15 Frontend-LDO spare contacts remain, too few for the rail enable/power-good pair plus the bottom chips' four DISC_OUT lines.
  • SYS-65The four V6 clock swaps belong on the Frontend: there each pair routes in 2-13 mm, while on FPGA-LDO A they need the same scarce space as the new lines and the ASIC3 swap has no path.
  • SYS-82Nothing on the Frontend blocks the V6 change set: all 20 nets route together with 28 ground stacks and need no layer, outline or ball change.
  • SYS-44The only ASIC-board Gerber set in the workspace is an older one-chip design with no pass-through lanes.
  • SYS-45The stimulation rails are set to +/-10.0 V, but no ASIC supply absolute maximum is on record.
  • SYS-50The ASIC rails come up in a fixed order, ±10 V last at about 0.25-0.5 s, and no required order is known.
  • SYS-62Pin map V6 gives the top chips their own SPI latch line with no pull-down, so it floats for about 7 s at every power-up while the bottom chips' latch is held low by R18.
  • SYS-51The Frontend pin file leaves drive strength and slew rate of the shared ASIC outputs on Vivado defaults.
  • SYS-77None of the 88 ASIC-to-FPGA input lines has a pull resistor on any board or in the pin file, so a missing or unpowered ASIC board leaves its returned clocks and data inputs floating.
  • SYS-08Three 0805 capacitors under the Frontend can be 1.45 mm tall in the 1.5 mm gap, leaving 0.05 mm worst case.
  • SYS-13Each stacked ASIC board's J1 pin tail lands on the J1 below and holds two DF40 joints about 0.24 mm open.
  • SYS-84Pin map V6 is built into every board but is not yet approved, and it differs from the plan in which wires are new.
  • SYS-05No CAD exists for the far ASIC board that must drive the T_ lanes, so half the J3/J4 pin map is unchecked.
  • SYS-04Each stack has four stimulation SPI data pairs for eight ASICs, so paired near and far chips get one stream.
  • SYS-24Ten ASIC-board pass-through vias are open holes inside or at the edge of DF40 pads, so solder can wick away.

Blockers and major items

  • AB-03All 119 AGND F.Cu-to-In1 laser vias on each board use 0.25 mm pads on 0.15 mm holes, some sit close to the edge or to other holes, and PCBWay, the fabricator now chosen, has not confirmed them.
  • AB-04On each board 40 via rings lie within 0.25 mm of the routed edge (closest 0.10 mm, hole 0.15 mm), and 19 rail vias touch their pulled-back plane only at its edge; 18 of these are the only path to the rail for one die pad each.
  • AB-05Hole-to-plane gaps (0.127 mm), 3 mil tracks, 0.05 mm via rings and hole pairs are below PCBWay's published normal limits for ten layers, and the KiCad rules equal the drawn values, so DRC cannot flag them.
  • AB-06In every stacked pair the upper board's 24 under-die capacitors leave only 1.00 mm (worst case) above the lower board for mask, die attach, die and probe base, not the 1.15 mm in A-1.
  • AB-09The ASIC boards carry no board name and the DF40 has no key, so two boards of one kind tie 22-24 chip outputs together and a near board turned 180° on its FPGA-LDO port puts the ±8 V stimulation rails on chip outputs.
  • AB-11The ASIC designer chose ENEPIG at PCBWay, but PCBWay has not confirmed ENEPIG together with laser vias on a 0.8 mm ten-layer board.
  • AB-12Nothing in the V4 package tells the bonding house where, which way or how to bond the dies: there is no die outline, centre, orientation mark or V4 bond diagram, and no wire, attach or encapsulation specification.
  • AB-13The 0.1016 mm bond fingers are below two published wire-bond guides and were bonded only on the one-die June board, and V4's 33 shared middle fingers (0.378 mm long) each need two opposed stitches with about 0.19 mm of finger per stitch, which nobody has shown.
  • AB-14The opened finger rows leave bare ±8 V stimulation copper 0.08-0.10 mm apart, so an encapsulant is required, but the rows end 0.41-0.50 mm from three routed edges, which leaves no room for a dam to hold it.
