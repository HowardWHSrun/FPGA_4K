At the XEM / BRK joint

The selected eight conductors per bank go to the cable only; matching BRK contacts are open to avoid high-speed stubs. R9 and R10 preserve physical routes across all 80 MC1 plus all 80 MC2 contacts. MC2 1–4 and 5–8 are VDCIN and DGND respectively, but their traces and current capacity remain unqualified. R10 connects all twelve selected signal pairs; power/JTAG branches, returns and other board connections remain unfinished.

ENGINEERING STATUS

What this drawing proves, and what remains to check.

Mapped in the KiCad R7 schematic

Three 19-contact ports, MC3 bank 226/225/224 pins, separate JTAG probe paths, isolated matching BRK GTY contacts and MC2 input-power contacts. The logical netlist and ERC checks passed. R10 physically connects all 160 MC1/MC2 paths and all twelve selected GTY pairs. Its independent contact audit passed. Branch-power/JTAG circuits, returns and other board connections remain incomplete.

Needs PCB and assembly verification

Actual mating footprints, orientation, stack height, fansink/bracket clearance, impedance and loss, GTY impedance and pair geometry, return paths, 12 V protection and load budget, shield/ESD, and Vivado GTY link proof. R10 has 97 unconnected items and 19 schematic-parity findings. Its physical DRC is clear under provisional HDI rules; this does not qualify the manufacturing process or establish a complete link. R9 and R8 remain separate.

USB capture still needs a rate test

Three boards stream at once, but the aggregate channel count is still to be confirmed. The actual total sample payload plus framing must fit sustained XEM USB, host and storage performance with an overflow policy.

PCIe is an alternate configuration

The J6 card edge shares GTY banks 224/225 used by two cables here. Keeping the possibility of PCIe later means a different population or adapter mode, not simultaneous PCIe operation in this layout.

The custom µHDMI cable assigns 12 V to contact 19, which is a nonstandard use of an HDMI connector. Do not mate it with ordinary HDMI equipment. Each of the three branches needs an explicit protected source, cable/contact current qualification and a power budget for its entire FPGA, routing and ASIC assembly.
