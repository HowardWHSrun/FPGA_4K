03 / Beyond this PCB

System items still needed

These are needed to build and test the intended one-cable system but have no approved purchase quantity or exact part selection in the present PCB draft.

Fabrication and assembly

Eight-layer bare PCB, stencil, assembly consumables, mounting hardware and prototype spares. Stackup, impedance, drill construction and assembly clearances need supplier review.

Matching connector and cable

Carrier-side DF40 mate, custom passive micro-HDMI cable and strain relief. Confirm the exact J5 mating series and continuity of all 19 contacts and shell.

Power input and ASIC rails

Protected input path, current limiting, inrush and backfeed control; power distribution to the ASIC carriers. Source voltage, current budget and carrier rail design remain open.

GTP receiver path

Compatible receiver adapter, GTP reference clock, AC coupling, ESD treatment and verified serial-link firmware. The native J4 high-speed data contacts are not yet wired.

Bring-up tools

Compatible 1.8 V JTAG probe, J6 removable shunt, access fixture, rail measurement equipment and a qualified bring-up plan. J6 manual INIT_B hold is drawn but is not an automatic rail supervisor.

04 / Before ordering

Resolve the design choices

The 50T board is useful for review now. These items determine whether its candidates can become an orderable BOM.

  1. Confirm the power contract. Choose 5 V or 12 V, quantify full board and ASIC load, then revise J4 input protection, cable and rail calculations together.
  2. Close connector identities. Check J4 footprint and cable drawing; resolve J5’s native DF40C footprint against the later DF40T mating direction.
  3. Approve FPGA and passive order codes. Select the complete XC7A50T suffix and exact codes for every TBD line.
  4. Complete the GTP design. Assign J4 data contacts, reference clock, receiver, AC coupling and ESD, then route and verify the PCB.
  5. Qualify manufacturing and power. Close ERC/DRC, current and thermal budgets, startup and shutdown, measurements at the FPGA pins, cable tests and supplier stackup.
