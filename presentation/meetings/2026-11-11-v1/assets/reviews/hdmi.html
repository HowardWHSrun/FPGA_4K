HDMI-32Note

Frontend JTAG TMS runs on Micro-HDMI pin 2 (Utility), a wire some cables do not connect.

On every port, pin 2 of the Micro-HDMI (Type D) connector carries the JTAG TMS line to the Frontend. HDMI calls pin 2 the Utility (HEAC+) pin; it was reserved before HDMI 1.4 and is needed only for the Ethernet and Audio Return channel, so some cables may leave it unwired. Pin 15 (the HDMI CEC pin, here TDI) is also sometimes left out of cheap cables. If pin 2 is open, JTAG to that Frontend fails while power and data links still work; the board files cannot settle this, only a continuity test of the actual cables can.

HDMI-01Minor

The port current limit is only 80 mA above the high-case Frontend load; a cable loop near 1 ohm uses it up.

Each port switch (TPS2553) starts limiting at 630 mA at the earliest, set by a 37.4k resistor on its ILIM pin. The recorded high-case Frontend load is 0.55 A, so the margin at the board is about 80 mA. The Frontend's only 5 V load is a buck converter that draws constant power, so as resistance in the single cable +5 V wire (pin 19) lowers the far-end voltage, the port current rises; with a pin-19 loop of about 0.9-1.0 ohm the high case reaches the limit. The stimulation converter on the LDO board (LT3582-12) is not in the budget and adds at most about 0.07-0.08 A at 5 V, part of which the budget's 3.3 V reserve already covers.

HDMI-47Minor

One U700 feedback fault can drive FE_5V to about 11.9 V, past the 7 V port switches and the heads' 6.5 V input limit, and nothing on FE_5V clamps it.

U700 sets FE_5V only through R700/R701 on its FB pin. Its power-good output, which enables all eight ports, also measures FE_5V through FB, so the module has no overvoltage check that works without FB. An open R700, a grounded FB pin or a shorted high-side switch drives FE_5V to about 11.7-12 V; power-good then keeps the ports off or turns them off, but the eight TPS2553 switches (7 V maximum) are left holding that voltage, and a fault that appears while running reaches every head for about 1-3 ms before the switches open. A wrong divider value (R700 about 55-115 kOhm or R701 about 3.5-7.3 kOhm) is worse: U700 regulates normally, power-good stays high, and every head receives a steady 6.5-11.9 V against its 6.5 V input maximum.

HDMI-31Note

WAKE#, SMDAT and SMCLK are left unconstrained, so Vivado's default pull-down can hold PCIe WAKE# low.

WAKE# is the PCIe line a card pulls low to ask the host to wake up; SMDAT and SMCLK are the slot's SMBus (a slow two-wire management bus). All three reach FPGA bank 64 through 0 ohm resistors, and the pin constraint file leaves them unconstrained on purpose, so Vivado gives them its default internal pull-down. WAKE# has only a weak 100 kohm pull-up on the FPGA side (about 18 uA at 1.8 V), so the pull-down can hold it low, and level shifter U6 passes that low to the slot. SMCLK and SMDAT have stronger 4.75 kohm pull-ups, so they are not held low and see only a small extra load.
