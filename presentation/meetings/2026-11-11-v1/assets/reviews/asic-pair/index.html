Re-check, 11 Nov evening

Independent reviewers re-checked both V3 boards and the system with them; serious items were verified again.

SeverityIDFindingSuggested action
MajorR2L-02The 0.25 mm plane pull-back leaves 37 through-via rings on every inner layer (40 on F.Cu and In1) 0.10-0.25 mm from the routed edge, with hole walls 0.15 mm from it.
V3 clipped the inner plane outlines 0.25 mm inside the edge, and the plane fills now stop there. But the rail vias that drop from the outer finger columns sit right at the left and right edges, and every via keeps its unused ring on every layer. So AGND, REF_VCM, REF_VDD, VDD_FE, VDD10 and VSS10 still put copper 0.10 mm from the edge on all ten layers. The board's own edge rule is 0.1016 mm, so DRC cannot flag it.		Move the 42 edge vias inward to at least 0.35 mm hole-to-edge (or get the fabricator's written yes for the present values), and set min_copper_edge_clearance to the fabricator's number so DRC checks it.
MajorR2S-04Nothing on the V3 boards stops or shows a wrong stack: a 180 degree turn or two boards of one kind puts +/-10 V on ASIC outputs or ties 22-24 outputs together.
The top and bottom V3 boards have the same outline, the same connector positions, no key and no board name on the silkscreen, but they are no longer interchangeable. There are now three wrong ways to stack them: turned 180 degrees, two bottom boards, or two top boards. A turn maps pin n to pin 81-n, which puts VDD10 (+10 V, pin 36) on pin 45, a bottom chip's CLK32MHZ output, and VSS10 (-10 V, pin 54) on pin 27, a data output. Two bottom boards tie 24 outputs together (22 odd-row lanes and DISC_OUT on pins 3/5) and leave two ASICs unread; two top boards tie the 22 even-row outputs.		Before ordering the pair, add large NEAR/FAR (or BOTTOM/TOP) text on both faces of each board and an orientation mark visible after mating, and decide on a mechanical key that lets only a far board sit on a near board.
MinorR2E-03The top and bottom boards carry no marking at all, so two boards of the same kind can be stacked and drive 22 output lines against each other.
Electrically the pair only works as one bottom board plus one top board. Two bottom boards on one port tie two chip outputs together on every odd-row data, READ, SYNC, CLK32 and DISC_OUT line and leave ASIC3/4 unread; two top boards do the same on the 22 even-row T_ lines. The two boards have the same outline and the same connectors, and neither file has any text on any layer, so nothing tells them apart. Swapping the order (top board plugged in first) is harmless: both boards pass all 80 pins, so only the ASIC numbering of the two boards swaps.		Add large copper or silkscreen text 'ASIC TOP (far) V3' and 'ASIC BOTTOM (near) V3' plus a date code on both faces, and write the stacking rule into the bring-up note.
MinorR2E-04Cutting the top chips' DISC_OUT left those four pads with no copper at all, while the ASIC designer has not decided whether DISC_OUT should be grounded or kept for testing.
V3 removed the output contention on pins 3/5 by disconnecting the top chips' DISC_OUT. That is correct for contention. But the designer's pin sheet says DISC_OUT might be shorted to ground or used for testing, and the ASIC reference records its termination as undecided. On the top board the two pads now have no track, via or pad of any kind, so neither grounding nor probing is possible without a respin. The bottom chips' DISC_OUT still reach pins 3/5, which are open on both FPGA-LDO boards.		Change the V3 question to 'must DISC_OUT be grounded, and is it needed for test?'; meanwhile add a DNP 0201 pad from each top DISC_OUT finger to ground, or route them to spare pins 4 and 6, which the bottom board already passes through.
MinorR2E-05After the 0.25 mm plane pull-back, 35 rail vias per board still sit 0.15-0.25 mm from the routed edge, 28 of them under JLCPCB's 0.2 mm, including VDD10 and VSS10.
The V3 pull-back moved the inner plane fills back from the edge, but the fan-out vias of the side bond fingers did not move. Their copper rings and barrels stay 0.152-0.248 mm from the routed outline on both boards. They carry VDD10, VSS10, REF_VCM, REF_VDD, VDD_FE and AGND, and each still joins its plane right at the pulled-back edge. KiCad passes them only because the board's own edge rule is 0.1016 mm.		Ask the fabricator for a written yes on vias 0.15 mm from a routed edge, or move the 28 vias inward by 0.05 mm, and set the board edge rule to 0.2 mm so KiCad reports them.
MinorR2E-06The merged bond-finger mask openings now leave VDD10 and VSS10 copper bare 0.08-0.10 mm apart, about 19-20 V across, on both boards.
V3 replaced the per-finger mask openings with one opening per finger row. Inside the rows the +10 V and -10 V fingers are 0.102 mm apart, and a VSS10 track enters the opening 0.082 mm from the VDD10 finger between the two dies. These are bare ENEPIG surfaces at almost 20 V difference on a board worn on an animal's head. The spacing is set by the die pad pitch, so the protection has to come from the encapsulant.		Make the encapsulant cover the whole opened finger rows a written requirement for the bonding house, and shorten the VSS10 track at (135.07-135.21, 76.51) so its corner stays under mask.
MinorR2L-04JLCPCB does not offer the provisional ENEPIG finish, so the bondable finish and the via build have to be bought from the same, different fabricator.
V3 sets the board finish to ENEPIG for wire bonding. JLCPCB publishes HASL, lead-free HASL, ENIG with 1 or 2 microinches of gold, and OSP; it lists neither ENEPIG nor soft gold. PCBWay's standard table lists ENEPIG. The bonding house has not yet named the finish and gold thickness it needs, so the fabricator choice is open and is tied to R2L-01.		Get the bonding house's finish and gold-thickness requirement in writing, then pick one fabricator that offers both that finish and the via build, and put both in the order notes.
MinorR2L-05Only the 38 converted vias under the dies are marked filled and capped; 170 other vias inside the finger rings and two vias in the C6/C50 0201 pads stay open, and an Excellon drill set carries no fill data.
The record says the under-die vias are filled and capped, but that covers only the 38 vias V3 converted. Each die area still has 83-87 open, tented vias (through, F.Cu-In1 and F.Cu-In8). The two 0201 decoupling capacitors near J4 keep an open via in a pad. KiCad writes the fill and cap marks only into Gerber-format drill output, not into the Excellon files usually sent.		Mark every via inside the finger rings and in C6/C50 filled and capped, or write 'fill and cap all vias' on the order, and send the filling/capping Gerbers or a drawing with it.
MinorR2L-06The new finger-row mask openings end 0-0.05 mm from 93 (top) / 91 (bottom) fan-out via rings, so normal mask misregistration will expose open vias beside bond fingers of other nets.
V3 opened the mask over each finger row with a 0.05 mm margin. The fan-out vias sit only 0.076-0.14 mm beyond the finger ends, so the mask left over them is 0-0.05 mm wide. Four rings already reach into an opening, and 61-63 vias have less than 0.05 mm of mask; 44-46 of the near vias carry a different net from the nearest finger. Inside the openings only fingers and their own lead-in traces are exposed, plus three slivers under 0.005 mm2. KiCad's mask-bridge allowance on U01 hides the one different-net ring that is already inside.		Ask the bonding house whether exposed via rings at the finger ends are acceptable; if not, trim the opening margin to 0 on the via side, move the four intruding vias, and fill and cap the vias next to the openings.
MinorR2S-06Both V3 boards keep the generic J1 side pin at the same spot, so in every pair the upper tail sits 0.235 mm into the lower pin and holds the DF40 joint that far open.
J1, a one-pin AGND right-angle header with a through-hole tail, is on both V3 boards at the same place, so in each stacked pair the upper board's tail comes down onto the lower board's bent lead. With 1.6 mm boards the overlap is 0.235 mm, about half the DF40's 0.45 mm effective mating length (the travel over which the contacts still touch), before any tolerance. The same pin clears the FPGA-LDO A / router top by 0.55 mm under the near board of the top-side port, and the near board of the underside port has 0.23 mm between its J1 body and the FPGA-LDO underside. The pin still has no part number, so the real tail length is unknown.		Fit J1 on only one board of each pair, or name a short-tail pin (or trim the tail to 0.5 mm) in the assembly notes, and give J1 a part number.
NoteR2L-07The top board's In4 re-route meets clearance and angle rules; six net pairs run closer than 3W for 6.8 mm, three lines cross the In5 split, and 50 track ends sit 2-6 micrometres off their via centres.
In4 carries 24 nets, 109 segments, 125.17 mm, all 0.0762 mm wide, only 0/45/90 degree segments, no acute corners and no T-junctions. Closest other-net copper is 0.0767 mm (REF_VCM pour). Six pairs of different nets run parallel with centre spacing under 3W (0.229 mm). Three lines change reference across the VDD10/VDD_DRIVER split on In5, as the record says. 25 tracks end 0.0018-0.0059 mm from their via centre; KiCad's track_not_centered_on_via check is set to ignore, so DRC does not show them.		None needed before ordering; snap the 25 track ends to their via centres the next time In4 is edited.
NoteR2L-08Every inner plane is one piece, but some plane regions hang on necks narrower than 0.25 mm; the narrowest, 0.12-0.15 mm, is on VSS10 (In6) of the top board.
An opening test (shrink then regrow the fill) splits off small regions that join the rest only through necks below 0.25 mm. They are between antipads near the connector and under the dies. All are wide enough to etch on an inner layer, and the rail currents are milliamps.		No action.
