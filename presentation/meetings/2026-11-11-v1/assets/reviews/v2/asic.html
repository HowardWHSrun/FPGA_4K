Do not order yet: 2 blockers, 12 major items

The ASIC board exists only as the near board of each stacked pair and has no fabrication package of its own, so it cannot be ordered as drawn. The blockers are ASIC-01 (no far board, and two stacked copies would drive the same 24 output wires) and ASIC-02 (the blind and buried via set has no workable lamination sequence); twelve major items follow, chiefly the missing and unspecified decoupling (ASIC-05, ASIC-06), the wire-bond finish and pads (ASIC-07, ASIC-13), the undecided thickness (ASIC-08) and the die and probe fit (ASIC-11, ASIC-12). First design the far-board variant and agree a buildable stack (thickness, via spans, bondable finish) with the fabricator, then restore the decoupling with real values and make a fabrication package from the current board.

ASIC-01Blocker

Only the near ASIC board of each stacked pair exists; stacking two copies would tie chip outputs together.

Each FPGA-LDO port (J3 or J4) carries two stacked ASIC boards, a near board and a far board. On this board J3 and J4 are wired straight through, both chips drive odd-row 'direct' pins, and the 22 T_ pins only pass through to the next board with no chip on them. The far board must therefore be a different design whose chips drive the T_ pins, and no such design exists in the workspace. Stacking two copies of this board ties two chips' outputs to the same wires (output contention, where two drivers fight over one line) and leaves the T_ lines undriven.

Where
ASIC board J3 (DF40C-80DS, bottom) and J4 (DF40C-80DP, top). Chip 1 (U01_BCD1): D<1..8> on pins 79, 77, 75, 73, 43, 41, 39, 37; READ 35; CLK32MHZ 45; SYNC 47; DISC_OUT 5. FPGA-LDO A and Option B router: J4 even pins to ASIC3/4, J3 even pins to ASIC7/8; pins 3 and 5 open.
If left alone
Only 4 of 8 ASICs (2,048 of 4,096 recording channels) per Frontend can work, and ASIC3/4/7/8 return nothing. Stacking four copies of this file gives fighting outputs and lost data; damage is possible but cannot be confirmed, because no file documents the ASIC output-stage type.
Suggested action
Get the far ('Top') board file from the ASIC designer, or decide to make it, and check before ordering that its chips drive only the T_ pins and leave pins 3/5 open. Do not order four copies of this file; mark the second board of each pair as not designed.

ASIC-02Blocker

The ASIC board's via set has 28 crossing span pairs, so no normal lamination sequence can build it.

The board uses one via, 0.15 mm drill with 0.25 mm pad, in 17 layer spans: from L1 down to every inner layer and from every inner layer to L10. Sequential lamination (pressing the board in stages and drilling between presses) can only make spans that nest or do not overlap; here 28 span pairs cross, for example 1-9 against 2-10, so at least one via of each pair would need controlled-depth drilling (drilling from the outside to a set depth). That works only to about 1:1 depth to width, and these holes reach 9.6:1. Two same-net via pairs also sit closer than the board's hole spacing minimum, which would need filled and capped vias that the board does not ask for.

Where
Whole ASIC board, all layers. 87 of the 133 non-through vias other than 1-2 sit in the J3/J4/capacitor band (y 67.4-73.5 mm). Examples: REF_VCM 1-5 and VDD_DRIVER 6-10 at (136.01, 68.44), 0.070 mm apart; DGND 9-10 and VDD_FE 1-3 at (134.45, 68.19), 0.068 mm apart; same-net pairs VDD_ADC 1-7/7-10 and REF_VDD 1-4/4-10.
If left alone
A fabricator will reject the order or quote it as any-layer HDI with a different thin-dielectric, laser-drilled, filled stack, which matches neither the drawn 0.15 mm drilled vias nor the 0.1825 mm cores. Even the 229 through vias at 0.15 mm on 1.6 mm (10.5:1) are high difficulty at PCBWay.
Suggested action
Ask the fabricator which spans and drill sizes it can build on this stack. Then reduce the set to the spans it accepts (for example through vias plus shallow 1-2 and 9-10): 170 of the 272 non-through vias convert to through directly, and the other 102 need local moves.

ASIC-04Major

Each near/far ASIC pair shares one SPI data stream, so eight chips get only four configuration streams.

Each ASIC chip loads its gains, reference DAC settings and stimulation tables through its own serial (SPI) data lines, SPI_DINL and SPI_DINR. The ASIC board passes no T_ copy of these lines and leaves its spare pins open, so a far board can only reach the near board's SPI wires, and FPGA-LDO A, the Option B router and the Frontend carry only four data pairs. Each far chip therefore gets exactly the same configuration, stimulation tables included, as the near chip below it, and SPI_LATCH and CHIP_RESET are shared by all eight chips. The project's earlier 117-signal baseline had six data streams and a clock per board; the dropped nets are still marked 'to confirm'.

Frontend: STIM_CHIP1..4_SPI_DL/DR only.

ASIC-05Major

The ASIC board places 18 of its 50 schematic decoupling capacitors, two per rail, 5.9-10.8 mm from the dies.

Decoupling capacitors are small local charge stores that keep a supply steady when a chip draws current in fast steps. The schematic has 50 but the board has 18, so each of the nine rails keeps exactly two, shared by both dies; 16 sit in a row on the far side of J4 from the dies and C6/C50 sit just east of J4. The cut came in the 2 Nov save that added J4 over the old capacitor area, and the designer's J3 spreadsheet lists exactly these 18, so it looks deliberate, but the schematic was not updated and no ASIC document gives a decoupling requirement. VDD_CGEN has no inner plane, so the far die's VDD_CGEN pin has no local storage.

Where
ASIC board, top side. Placed: C1, C2, C4, C5, C7, C14, C18, C19, C25, C26, C33, C34, C41, C42, C46, C48 at y 68.1 mm, x 129.6-140.3; C6 (145.53, 70.92) and C50 (145.55, 71.79). J4 pads span y 69.65-72.36; die pads y 75.36-83.96. Schematic-only: C3, C8-C13, C15-C17, C20-C24, C27-C32, C35-C40, C43-C45, C47, C49. VDD_CGEN at U01_BCD1.

ASIC-06Major

No ASIC-board capacitor has a value, voltage rating or part number, including four 0201 parts on ±10 V rails.

All 50 schematic capacitors carry only the symbol name 'C_Small_US' as their value, with no voltage rating, MPN (manufacturer part number), LCSC number or datasheet, so the board cannot be quoted or assembled from these files. Of the 18 capacitors on the board, four 0201 parts sit on the ±10 V stimulation rails; the schematic adds eight more that are not placed. An unspecified 0201 part could be rated 6.3 V or 10 V, which would put it at or above 100 % of its rating, and ceramic capacitors lose much of their capacitance under DC bias. 0201 parts rated 16-25 V exist, so the gap is the missing rating, not the package.

Where
ASIC board C1-C50 (schematic), 18 on the board. On the ±10 V rails on the board: C18, C19 (/VDD10 to /AGND) and C25, C26 (/VSS10 to /AGND), 0201, top side at y about 68.1 mm. Schematic-only on these rails: C15-C17, C20 (VDD10) and C21-C24 (VSS10).
If left alone
The assembler cannot quote. A 10 V-rated part on a 10.0 V rail runs at about 100 % of its rating, with large capacitance loss and higher failure risk.
Suggested action
Get the intended values from the ASIC designer and fill in value, rating (at least 16 V, preferably 25 V, for C18, C19, C25 and C26) and MPN in the schematic fields.

How this review was done

Each topic was examined by an independent AI reviewer (Claude) working from the saved KiCad files, fresh KiCad checks run on 11 November 2026, and the manufacturers’ documents. Every blocker and major finding was then re-checked by two further reviewers who tried to prove it wrong, with a third when they disagreed. Lower-severity findings were re-checked once. A review lead then looked for anything nobody had covered and sent out follow-up checks.

Nothing has been built, powered or measured. A finding marked “needs a measurement” or “needs a vendor answer” cannot be settled from files. A clean KiCad check means only that KiCad’s rules pass. This review changed no design file.
