R2S-03Major

The V3 boards are 1.6 mm, so each head stack is about 17.0 mm tall, not 13.8 mm, and the four probe rows are 3.1 / 6.2 / 3.1 mm apart, not 2.3 / 5.4 / 2.3 mm.

Both V3 board files are ten-layer, 1.6 mm. The project record says the ASIC board will be 0.8 mm (6 Nov), and the print models, the deck's head view on the sheep and the Option A fit use 0.8 mm. With four 1.6 mm ASIC boards each stack grows by about 3.1-3.2 mm and 2.3-2.9 g. Each board's dies sit on its top face and its probes leave the same edge (y 100.0 in the FPGA-LDO frame), so the four probe rows of a stack spread over 12.4 mm instead of 10.0 mm. The V3 record lists thickness as needing input; until it is decided, the models used to judge fit on the animal and the probe plan do not match the board files.

R2S-05Major

Each port's 44 chip outputs return through two DGND contacts, and the far chips' outputs return through the REF_VDD and VDD_DRIVER planes, AGND contacts 42/44 and NT1.

Each 80-pin port has AGND only on pins 42/44 and DGND only on 46/48. With two real boards stacked, the 44 outputs of four chips return through the two DGND contacts at the FPGA-LDO joint, and the far board's 22 outputs also cross a second joint with the same four returns. On the far (top) board those outputs, including both returned CLK32MHZ clocks, run 4-7.5 mm on In4 between the REF_VDD plane (In3) and the VDD10/VDD_DRIVER plane (In5). Their return current reaches ground mainly through rail capacitors that go to AGND (and plane-to-plane capacitance), then leaves the stack on AGND pins 42/44 and crosses to GND at NT1 on the FPGA-LDO board, through the reference and analog ground of every chip in the port. SYS-41 asked that the second board keep clock lanes on a DGND-referenced layer; V3 does not. The six spare pins 2/4/6/8/24/58 are now nets on both V3 boards but no-connect on FPGA-LDO A and the router, so using them as extra grounds later needs an FPGA-LDO change. The files cannot show how large the effect is.

Where
V3 top board In4: T_CLK32MHZ 5.06 mm, T_CLK32MHZ(2) 6.66 mm, T_D<1..8>, T_D<1..8>(2), T_READ, T_SYNC and their (2) copies 3.97-7.47 mm each; In3 /REF_VDD, In4 /REF_VCM, In5 /VDD10 + /VDD_DRIVER. J3/J4 AGND 42/44, DGND 46/48, spares SPARE_P2..P58. FPGA-LDO A and router J3/J4 pins 2/4/6/8/24/58 unconnected; NT1 on FPGA-LDO A at (100.58, 121.0). Option B adds J5 (GND 3/65, AGND 62).
If left alone
Digital edge current flows in the ASICs' reference supply and analog ground and through NT1, which can add noise to all eight recordings and disturb the returned clocks; the cheap fix on the spare pins is lost once FPGA-LDO A is ordered.
Suggested action
Before ordering FPGA-LDO A, ask the ASIC designer whether pins 2/4/6/8/24/58 may be DGND on the ASIC pair and, if so, tie them to GND on FPGA-LDO A and the router; on the top board, keep the T_ lanes over DGND or AGND (B.Cu/In8 or F.Cu/In1 side) rather than In4; measure AGND-DGND noise at bring-up.

SYS-01Major

Each cable's one GT lane must carry eight ASICs, but the only recorded line rate (1.25 Gb/s) counts one ASIC.

Each Frontend sends all its recording data over one GT (multi-gigabit transceiver) lane, U1 TX0, through its HDMI cable. That lane must carry all eight ASICs on the Frontend: 64 data lines, 2.048 Gb/s raw or 1.536 Gb/s once the 12-bit words are packed. The only recorded line rate, about 1.25 Gb/s with an 800 ps bit, was worked out from one ASIC per port; after 8b/10b coding (a line code that sends 10 bits for every 8 data bits) it leaves only 1.0 Gb/s. A workable lane is 2.5 Gb/s (packed data) or 3.125 Gb/s (raw frames), and no line rate, framing, cable grade or loss budget has been set or checked at that rate.

Where
Frontend U1 (XC7A35T-2CSG325I) GTP TX0 balls H2 (P) / H1 (N); TX1-TX3 unconnected, RX balls on GND. J3 pin 5 = GTP_TX0_P, pin 3 = GTP_TX0_N (swap intended, TXPOLARITY=1). Micro-HDMI cable, about 1.8 m. HDMI Extension Board J610-J617 pins 3/5 -> ESD U801-U808 -> AC capacitors C745-C760 -> MC3 GTY RX quads 224/226 (rec_rx[0..7]).
If left alone
If cables or firmware are planned around 1.25 Gb/s, the link cannot carry eight ASICs. At 2.5-3.125 Gb/s a thin 1.8 m Micro-HDMI cable with its ESD parts and connectors may not give an open eye, which shows up as bit errors and lost recording frames on some ports.
Suggested action
Record the lane rate and framing (2.5 Gb/s with packed 12-bit data, or 3.125 Gb/s with raw frames) and reopen the HDMI-board items tied to the rate. Run an IBERT eye and loss check over one real cable at 1.25-1.56 GHz Nyquist before buying eight sets.

SYS-02Major

High-load stacks reach the 637 mA port current limit on a 1.8 m cable with 30-32 AWG or thinner +5 V wire.

Each stack (Frontend, FPGA-LDO board and four ASIC boards) gets all its power through pin 19 of its Micro-HDMI cable, a single +5 V wire. The Frontend buck U16 draws close to constant power, so when the cable voltage drops its current rises. With a perfect cable the high-load case needs about 0.55-0.58 A, below the port switch's 637 mA minimum limit, but once the cable loop resistance passes about 0.9-1.3 ohm (30-32 AWG at 1.8 m) it reaches that limit, and at 34-36 AWG there is no usable operating point. The expected load works on every gauge; the Frontend power has never been estimated with Vivado/XPE and the chosen cable's wire gauge is unknown, so a power estimate and a measured cable loop resistance are needed.

Return on pins 4/7/10/13/16 and the shell.

SYS-03Major

SYS-04Major

Each stack has four stimulation SPI data pairs for eight ASICs, so paired near and far chips get one stream.

Each ASIC has its own SPI_DL and SPI_DR inputs (the serial lines that load its configuration and stimulation tables), and the ASIC designer's pin sheet lists them under 'Not Shared'. The Frontend brings out only four pairs (STIM_CHIP1..4_SPI_DL/DR), and the ASIC board passes SPI_DINR/SPI_DINL straight from J3 to J4 with no T_ copy, so the far board's chip in each position receives the near chip's stream. With SPI_CLK and SPI_LATCH also shared and no chip address in the stream, each pair gets the same gain, DAC settings and stimulation table, and a far chip cannot be left idle. The sheet groups the pins but does not forbid sharing, so this is a decision for the ASIC designer and the board's owner; if a far-board variant instead moved SPI to the spare pins 2/4/6/8/24/58, those pins are open on both FPGA-LDO designs and the far chips would get no SPI at all.

Where
Frontend U1 C11/G14 (CHIP1), C14/C16 (CHIP2), V7/V6 (CHIP3), N17/N18 (CHIP4). FPGA-LDO A and Option B router: J4 pins 57/59 (STIM_CHIP1) and 23/25 (CHIP2), J3 pins 57/59 (CHIP3) and 23/25 (CHIP4); J3/J4 pins 2, 4, 6, 8, 24, 58 open. ASIC board J3 = J4 on SPI_DINR 57, SPI_DINL 59, SPI_DINR(2) 23, SPI_DINL(2) 25. Pairs: CHIP1 -> ASIC1 + ASIC3, CHIP2 -> ASIC2 + ASIC4, CHIP3 -> ASIC5 + ASIC7, CHIP4 -> ASIC6 + ASIC8.
If left alone
Gain, DAC settings and stimulation tables cannot be set per chip for four of the eight ASICs in each stack, and paired chips stimulate the same pattern. Changing this after the Frontend and LDO boards are made needs a spin of both.
Suggested action
Ask the ASIC designer whether paired chips may share one SPI stream. If not, route eight more SPI data lines (four per ASIC port) over the spare connector pins on the Frontend and the FPGA-LDO before ordering either.

SYS-06Major

Option B J5 carries 108 signals with only three return contacts: GND on pins 3 and 65 and AGND on pin 62.

J5, the 120-pin DF40 board-to-board connector between the Option B router and LDO board, uses every contact: 108 single-ended 1.5 V signals, 9 ASIC rails and only three returns. A fast signal's return current flows back along the nearest ground path, and 51 of the 108 signals are more than 5 mm from a GND contact, so about 80 lines switching together at 32 MHz can cause crosstalk and ground bounce. SYS-52Note

Returned CLK32MHz lines have no series resistor, so a strong ASIC driver could overshoot the FPGA input limit.

Each ASIC drives its 32 MHz recording clock point-to-point to an FPGA clock pin with no series resistor, and the FPGA captures data on the falling edge. Overshoot is the voltage swinging above the supply; AMD limits how far above the 1.5 V bank supply an input may go and for how long. In a linear model of the far-chip (ASIC3) path the edge stays clean, but a strong, fast driver peaks near 2.5 V, about 1 V over the bank, beyond AMD's allowance, while a 30 ohm, 1 ns driver stays under about 1.7 V. The files cannot settle this because the ASIC output drive is unknown; a probe measurement at bring-up or the ASIC designer's driver data would.

Where
Far chip: ASIC3_CLK32MHz_Out from the far board (no CAD) through the near ASIC board's T_CLK32MHZ (J3.70/J4.70), FPGA-LDO Option A J4.70 to J2.35, Frontend J2.35 to U1.P4. Near chip: ASIC1 CLK32MHZ (J4.45), Option A J4.45 to J2.59, Frontend J2.59 to U1.R2. The same applies to ASIC2 and ASIC4-8.
If left alone
Probably works. With a strong, fast ASIC driver the FPGA clock inputs would see overshoot beyond AMD's limit on every cycle.

How this review was done

Every blocker and major finding was then re-checked by two further reviewers who tried to prove it wrong, with a third when they disagreed. Lower-severity findings were re-checked once. Nothing has been built, powered or measured. A finding marked “needs a measurement” or “needs a vendor answer” cannot be settled from files. A clean KiCad check means only that KiCad’s rules pass. This review changed no design file.
