HDMI-01Minor

The port current limit is only 80 mA above the high-case Frontend load; a cable loop near 1 ohm uses it up.

Each port switch (TPS2553) starts limiting at 630 mA at the earliest, set by a 37.4k resistor on its ILIM pin. The recorded high-case Frontend load is 0.55 A, so the margin at the board is about 80 mA. The Frontend's only 5 V load is a buck converter that draws constant power, so as resistance in the single cable +5 V wire (pin 19) lowers the far-end voltage, the port current rises; with a pin-19 loop of about 0.9-1.0 ohm the high case reaches the limit. The stimulation converter on the LDO board (LT3582-12) is not in the budget and adds at most about 0.07-0.08 A at 5 V, part of which the budget's 3.3 V reserve already covers.

HDMI Extension Board V37: U701-U708 (TPS2553DBVR) pin 5 ILIM with R711-R718 (37.4k); PORT1_5V-PORT8_5V from U70x pin 6 to J610-J617 pin 19; FE_5V = 5.02 V set by R700 40.2k / R701 10k. Frontend: J3.19 VIN_5V feeds only U16 (MPM3833C). FPGA-LDO A: U1 LT3582EUD-12 pin 5 on /VIN_3V3 (J2.68-70), feeding U9 LT3042 (/ASIC_VDD10) and U10 LT3094 (/ASIC_VSS10).
If left alone
With the high-case load on a thin or long cable, the port goes into current limit, VIN_5V sags, U16 drops out and the Frontend and ASICs brown out or hiccup. The connector is not overrun: the limit stays under 0.8 A and in a hard short the switch thermal-cycles.
Suggested action
Measure the 5 V current of one full stack (start-up, recording, full stimulation) and the pin-19 loop resistance of the chosen cable; if the loop is above about 0.5-0.7 ohm, use shorter or heavier cables or the BOM-only change R700 = 42.2k (5.22 V).

HDMI-02Minor

The J4 barrel jack feeds the 7.5-15 V XEM8310 with no fuse, reverse-polarity or over-voltage protection.

J4 connects straight to +VDCIN, which feeds the XEM8310 module through MC2 and also feeds the 5 V module U700. J4 accepts any common 5.5/2.1 mm plug, so a 19-24 V laptop adapter or a centre-negative adapter goes directly into the XEM8310, which is specified for 7.5-15 V. The XEM8310's own barrel jack is on the same net, so plugging in both puts two adapters in parallel. This arrangement is copied from the Opal Kelly BRK8310 breakout, which has the same exposure; only the silkscreen marks the required supply.

HDMI-03Minor

U700 has thinner heat-spreading copper than TI recommends, and no thermal check exists for its high-case load.

The 5 V module U700 sheds its heat through 12 small vias into 15 um inner planes and small top-side ground areas; TI recommends 2 oz (about 70 um) outer copper and no less than 1 oz, and this board has 35 um outer copper. The L6 pour under it is split into six pieces, and only a 14.4 mm2 piece holds the 12 PGND vias. At the high-case load of 8 x 0.55 A it loses about 1.6 W, and with TI's reference thermal resistance the junction reaches about 93-103 C at 40-50 C ambient, 20-30 C below the 125 C limit. It gets close to 125 C only if all eight ports sit near their current limit together, which is a fault case. No thermal simulation or measurement exists.

Normal and high-case use should stay below 125 C. With all eight ports held near their current limit in a warm enclosure, the module may thermally limit and drop all eight ports at once.
Suggested action
Keep the recorded accept-and-measure handling: at bring-up, load all eight ports to the measured worst case in the intended enclosure and log the U700 case temperature (psi-JT is 1 C/W, so case is nearly junction); add airflow or a heat sink if it passes about 100 C.

HDMI-05Minor

U602 drives the FT232H TDO pin while the FT232H, in its default UART mode, also drives that pin.

The level shifter U602 (TXU0304) drives HOST_TDO from a push-pull output straight into FT232H pin 15 (ADBUS2), with no series resistor. U602 is enabled whenever a powered target is selected, and when the target's TDO is idle and floating, U602's weak input pull-down makes that output drive low. In its default asynchronous UART mode the FT232H uses ADBUS2 as the RTS# output, so two outputs fight until software switches the chip to MPSSE, its JTAG engine. The board fits a blank EEPROM; that a blank EEPROM leaves the chip in UART mode rests on secondary sources, so FTDI's own documents or a vendor answer should confirm it.

Where
HDMI Extension Board V37: U602 pin 5 (A4Y) on net HOST_TDO to U501 pin 15 (ADBUS2); R509 10k pull-up to FTDI3V3 is the only other part on the net; U602 pin 8 OE = DRIVE_ENABLE from U605 (TPS3808) pin 1, gated by the VREF window and the MASTER switch SW601; U505 93LC56BT-I/SN EEPROM.
If left alone
From first bring-up until the EEPROM sets a non-UART mode, and whenever the chip sits in UART mode with a target selected, both drivers short into each other. This stresses U602 and U501 beyond their datasheet limits, though it is not an immediate failure.
Suggested action
Confirm the ADBUS2 state with a blank EEPROM and after programming from FTDI's own datasheet or FTDI; if it is driven, add a 330 ohm to 1 kohm series resistor between U602.5 and U501.15, or keep SW601 OFF until the EEPROM is programmed.

HDMI-06Minor

Through a 2 m cable, Frontend JTAG works only up to about 5.5 MHz TCK, well below the common 15 MHz default.

In JTAG the target's TDO answer must get back to the FT232H within half a TCK clock period. The round trip adds the U602 level-shifter delay both ways, the cable both ways, the FPGA's clock-to-TDO time, a heavily loaded 225 mm TDO trunk, and the FT232H's input setup time. In the worst case with a 2 m cable that is about 91 ns, which limits TCK to about 5.5 MHz (about 6.2 MHz with a 1 m cable). Vivado's default for Digilent-type cables is 15 MHz; whether an FT232H programmed for this board gets that default was not confirmed.

Where
HDMI Extension Board V37: JTAG_TCK_1V8 (R601 22R) and JTAG_TDO_1V8 trunks from U602 to U610-U618; per-port 100R (R640-R671, e.g. R643 on HDMI1_JTAG_TDO); J610-J617 pins 17/18; cable; Frontend U1 F8/T8.
If left alone
At a 15 MHz TCK, scans or programming of a Frontend may fail intermittently or read wrong IDCODEs, which looks like a cable or board fault.
Suggested action
At bring-up, set the hw_server TCK to 3-6 MHz for Frontend targets and step it up while checking IDCODE reads; record the working limit per cable length.

HDMI-07Minor

U602 is a TXU0304 level shifter that moves the JTAG signals between 3.3 V and 1.8 V. A bypass capacitor is a small capacitor placed right at a chip's supply pin to supply the short current pulses drawn when its outputs switch. TI asks for one on each supply pin, as close to the device as possible, but the nearest capacitors here are 9.5 to 10.4 mm away and the 1.8 V supply has no plane, only 0.2-0.3 mm track. The capacitors sit where they were in V35, so V36 and V37 did not move them.

HDMI-08Minor

The U801-U816 ESD pads do not follow TI's DQA0010A pattern: signal pads are wider and ground pads narrower.

U801-U816 are TPD4E02B04 ESD protection arrays in a 1.0 x 2.5 mm, 10-pad USON package with 0.5 mm pitch. The copper gap between adjacent signal pads is 0.20 mm instead of TI's 0.30 mm, but the paste openings put about the same solder on the signal pads as TI's stencil, so the chance of a solder bridge rises only modestly. The ground pads get about half the paste TI shows.

HDMI-09Minor

L3 is about 9.5 % copper against about 93 % on L2 and L4, and no balancing or bow/twist limit is decided.

A multilayer board stays flattest when each layer's copper is matched by similar copper on its mirror layer about the board centre. L3 (In2.Cu) has no pour and is about 9.5 % copper, while L2, L4 and L5 are about 93 %, so L3 is unbalanced against L2 in its own core and against its mirror L4 across the centre prepreg; L1/L6 and L2/L5 are balanced. Uneven copper can make a board bow or twist during lamination and reflow, but the thin 0.5 oz inner copper and this common layer arrangement limit the effect, and the files cannot predict how much bow results. The order note invites JLC to propose balancing copper but sets no bow/twist limit, and no decision is recorded.

Where
HDMI Extension Board V37, L3 (In2.Cu) against L2 (In1.Cu) and L4 (In3.Cu), whole board. Most sensitive: the J6 card-edge tongue (y 149-162) and the XEM8310 connectors MC1/MC2/MC3 (x 147-192, y 67-133).
If left alone
If nothing is added, the board may bow more than expected, and a bowed card can seat poorly in the PCIe slot or strain the XEM8310 connector joints. How likely this is cannot be predicted from the files.
Suggested action
Decide before ordering: add a hatched DGND fill on L3 kept 1.0 mm from every pair, or tell JLC in writing that L3 thieving outside the 1.0 mm keep-out is wanted. In both cases ask JLC to state the bow/twist limit they will hold.

The V3 enclosure has no known inside air temperature, and an estimate puts it 15-49 C or more above the room.

The enclosure V3 record says its thermal behaviour has not been simulated or measured. The box is closed, has no fan and has only 11 slots in the keel floor (the keel is the lower pocket that holds the Thunderbolt adapter), yet it also holds the XEM8310 and the adapter. The U700 heat sinks are described only as 'as V2'. The current U700 (the 5 V power module) can safely deliver falls as the inside air gets warmer, so the files cannot fix a U700 current budget for the box; only a measured box-air temperature can.

Where
Enclosure V3 (inputs/hdmi/enclosure_V3/START_HERE.md, 'Power' and 'Open points'); U700 at the J4 corner of the HDMI Extension Board V37.
If left alone
Without a measured or simulated box-air temperature, the U700 current budget in the enclosure cannot be stated more tightly than the 40-60 C range used here.
Suggested action
Before relying on the 0.63-0.71 A per-port cases, run the board and XEM8310 in the printed box with a dummy 5 A FE_5V load and log the box air and U700 top temperature, or add a small fan or vents to the V3 concept.
