The Frontend passes KiCad's checks (0 unconnected, parity 0, 144 warnings that were all traced as harmless), and its ball map, connector pin maps and order files agree with each other; there is no blocker, but seven major items remain. First secure FE-06 and FE-05 parts and measure the FE-04 clearance, then decide FE-01, FE-03 and FE-07 before ordering.

FE-01Major

Output capacitance on MPM3833C rails exceeds the datasheet's 100 uF maximum, clearly so on VCCINT (186 uF).

The MPM3833C datasheet lists 10 uF minimum, 22 uF typical and 100 uF maximum output capacitance, measured at one test point (1.2 V out, 2 A) on engineering samples. The module uses constant-on-time control (each switching pulse lasts a fixed time), and too much output capacitance can make its load-step response unstable. U2 on VCCINT has 186 uF fitted, still about 140-150 uF after the capacitors lose value under DC voltage, and also has a 22 pF feed-forward capacitor (C3, output to FB) that the MPS circuit does not show. VCCAUX (144 uF nominal) is borderline after derating, while VCCO and VIN_3V3 are probably below 100 uF effective; MPS does not show that any of these combinations is stable.

Where
Frontend. U2 (VCCINT_1V0): C4 100u, C10 47u, C2 22u, C5/C6/C90 4.7u, C7/C8/C9/C11/C91/C92/C93 0.47u, CFF C3 22 pF; C89 100u is DNP. U3 (VCCAUX_1V8): C15/C59 47u, C13 22u, C16/C17/C94/C95/C96 4.7u, small parts, CFF C14. U4 (VCCO_1V5): C42 47u, C22 22u, 8 x 4.7u, 11 x 0.47u, CFF C23. U16 (VIN_3V3, no CFF): Frontend capacitors, SWITCHED_3V3 behind U10, and the FPGA-LDO board's /VIN_3V3 through J2 pins 68-70.
If left alone
VCCINT, and possibly VCCAUX, may ring or oscillate on load steps once the FPGA design runs. Start-up is not at risk (1.3 ms soft-start needs well under 1 A to charge these values).
Suggested action
Ask MPS to confirm U2 and U3 with these COUT values and the 22 pF CFF, or mark C4 not fitted so VCCINT drops below 100 uF; then do a load-step check on the first boards.

FE-02Major

High-case stack load at the 5 V pin comes within about 0.02-0.10 A of the 0.63 A minimum port current limit.

All Frontend and FPGA-LDO power enters on one HDMI contact, J3.19, and passes through U16. Each HDMI port on the HDMI Extension Board has a TPS2553 switch that holds current constant at its limit; U16 is a buck converter, so if its input sags it draws more current, which can brown out or cycle the stack. Built up from the project's per-rail estimate, the load is comfortably below the limit in the expected case but close to it in the high case with all stimulators on, and the LDO board's +/-12 V start-up surge will briefly hit the limit at every power-up. No Vivado power figure or measurement exists, so the files cannot settle this; a measurement on the first Frontend + FPGA-LDO stack can.

Where
Frontend J3.19 VIN_5V (TP14) -> U16 -> VIN_3V3, which also leaves on J2 pins 68-70 to the FPGA-LDO board; HDMI Extension Board TPS2553 port switches U701-U708 with ILIM resistors R711-R718 = 37.4k.
If left alone
In the high case, ports may enter current limit and the stack may brown out or fail to start. The limit cannot simply be raised, because its maximum is already near the J3 contact rating, so a fix could mean a cable, board or power-architecture change.
Suggested action
Get a Vivado report_power or XPE figure and measure the first Frontend + FPGA-LDO stack at TP14, including start-up with the +/-12 V converter, before fixing R711-R718 and ordering eight stacks.

FE-04Major

Three fitted 0805 capacitors under the Frontend leave 0.05 mm worst-case clearance to the FPGA-LDO board.

The DF40C connector pair sets a 1.5 mm gap between the Frontend bottom and the FPGA-LDO top. Three fitted 0805 bulk capacitors on the Frontend bottom are 1.25 +/- 0.2 mm thick, which leaves 0.25 mm at nominal and 0.05 mm at maximum thickness, before solder mask on the LDO board and connector seating tolerance (Hirose gives none). Contact loss on the connector is unlikely, because the DF40 still engages with 0.45 mm of mating length; the real risk is a capacitor that carries the mating force and cracks. The files cannot settle the true gap; a measurement on the first assembled pair can.

FE-06Major

Three out-of-stock parts are feedback resistors; building without them drives GTP rails above absolute max.

R7, R10 and R13 have no JLC stock and are the upper feedback resistors (output to FB pin) of U4, U5 and U6. If the order is built with these lines not placed, each lower resistor holds FB at 0 V and the regulator drives its output toward its input. MGTAVCC and MGTAVTT, the FPGA transceiver supplies, would then rise to about 1.35-1.4 V, above their 1.10 V and 1.32 V absolute maximums. VCCO_1V5 would rise to about 3.3 V; that is within the bank's 3.6 V absolute maximum, but it puts 3.3 V levels on 1.5 V lines to the ASIC boards, and U4's power-good stays low, holding U6 off and the FPGA in reset.

FE-08Minor

If a design drives FPGA ball M15 (EN_GTPWR) low, the FPGA resets itself and can reboot in a loop.

FPGA ball M15 turns on the 1.4 V transceiver (GT) supply U13; U13's power-good starts regulator U5 (MGTAVCC), and U5's power-good is on the same net that drives the reset supervisor U12's manual-reset input. U12 then pulls PROGRAM_B and INIT_B low, which erases the FPGA configuration. So a design that drives M15 low erases itself; M15 then floats, R58 turns GT power back on, U12 releases about 1.07 s later and the same design reloads, so it can repeat. Holding configuration off while a GT rail is bad is intended and required (the GT calibration runs only during configuration), so the only defect is that M15 cannot be used to switch GT power at runtime; no record states that M15 must stay high.

Where
Frontend: U1.M15 EN_GTPWR_1V5 (R58 4.99k to VCCO_1V5) -> U15 SN74AXC1T45 -> EN_GTPWR_3V3 (R57 10k to GND) -> U13.4 EN; U13.12 PG_GTP_1V4 -> U5.5 EN; U5.3 PG on BOOT_RAILS_PG_3V3 (with U4.12 PG, U6.5 EN, U12.3 MR, R9, TP10); U12.6 BOOT_RESET_N -> U11 (74AUP2G07) -> U11.6 FPGA_PROGRAM_B (U1.P10), U11.4 FPGA_INIT_B (U1.T10).
If left alone
A gateware default of 0 on M15, or a firmware attempt to power-cycle the GT link, turns the board into a reboot loop that looks like a power or flash fault at bring-up. GT power gating, the stated purpose of M15, cannot be used without a full reconfiguration.
Suggested action
Write 'M15 must be driven high or left undriven' into the XDC and the schematic note, and check that Vivado's default unused-pin pulldown (from memory, not verified) cannot pull M15 below U15's input threshold through R58 4.99k.

How this review was done

Each topic was examined by an independent AI reviewer (Claude) working from the saved KiCad files, fresh KiCad checks run on 11 November 2026, and the manufacturers’ documents. Every blocker and major finding was then re-checked by two further reviewers who tried to prove it wrong, with a third when they disagreed. Lower-severity findings were re-checked once. A review lead then looked for anything nobody had covered and sent out follow-up checks.

Nothing has been built, powered or measured. A finding marked “needs a measurement” or “needs a vendor answer” cannot be settled from files. A clean KiCad check means only that KiCad’s rules pass. This review changed no design file.
