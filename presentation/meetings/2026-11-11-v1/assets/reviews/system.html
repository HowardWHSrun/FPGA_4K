Settle before ordering the head boards 4

R2S-03Major

PCBWay has not confirmed ten layers in 0.8 mm for the ASIC boards.

Ten layers in 0.8 mm is outside PCBWay's standard range (https://www.pcbway.com/capabilities.html). If PCBWay refuses, the thickness, vias and stack height all change. The fit and print models already use 0.8 mm (set V3, 13 Nov).

What to do
Send the ASIC V7 stack and via table to PCBWay and get a written yes for ten layers in 0.8 mm with ENEPIG and laser vias.

SYS-31Major

The ASIC designer still owes the die and bond-loop height, the capacitor values and the VDD_CGEN level.

The bonded dies sit about 1.5 mm under the next ASIC board. If die, bond loops and encapsulant exceed about 1.4 mm (less with tall back-side capacitors), mating the next board crushes the bond wires. VDD_CGEN is supplied at 1.5 V; the earlier LDO design used 1.6 V. The ASIC board J3/J4 have no part number, so a taller DF40 could also be fitted.

What to do
Get die thickness, loop height, encapsulant height, capacitor values and the VDD_CGEN level from the ASIC designer; record a maximum height. Put the DF40 part numbers on ASIC J3/J4.
Includes
SYS-85 VDD_CGEN is supplied at 1.5 V, while the earlier LDO design on record used 1.6 V, and the meeting gave no level., SYS-30 ASIC board J3/J4 have no part number, and their land pattern also fits taller DF40 parts that alter the stack.

SYS-57Major

Panel break-off tabs sit beside mating parts on the Frontend and FPGA-LDO A.

The Frontend's south edge is 0.567 mm from the near ASIC board, and its panel puts two tabs on that edge; a tab nub can stop the 0.45 mm DF40 engagement. Four of FPGA-LDO A's six tabs are 0.9-1.3 mm from J1-J4, inside JLC's 1.5 mm tab keep-out (https://jlcpcb.com/blog/pcb-mouse-bites); snapping them bends the connector joints.

What to do
Move the Frontend's south tabs to another edge and order ±0.1 mm outline routing. Move FPGA-LDO A's tabs at least 1.5 mm from J1-J4.
Includes
SYS-71 The FPGA-LDO A order leaves break-off tab positions to JLC, although J1/J2 sit 0.88 mm from both long edges and only short edge stretches meet JLC's own tab keep-out.
Full entry
SYS-57 in the V3 list (evidence and history)
SYS-56Major

There is no grounding plan for eight stacks on one animal.

If the stacks share one head ground, supply return current (milliamps) runs through ASIC AGND and the recording reference, so load steps can put up to about 13 mV on a reference that records microvolt signals. The animal is also tied to the PC and 12 V adapter ground with no isolation or leakage rule.

What to do
Before ordering FPGA-LDO A, decide where the animal ground attaches (ASIC J1 is no longer fitted) and add a GND strap pad to each head board. Test two powered stacks in saline. Write down the earth and leakage rule for the rig.
Includes
SYS-11 No record sets a grounding rule for eight stacks on one animal, each tied to base ground by its own cable., SYS-78 The animal's reference electrode is tied to the host PC and 12 V adapter grounds, with no isolation and no leakage rule on record.

Settle before the first power-up 3

SYS-09Major

The connectors are unkeyed, so an ASIC board can mate turned 180°.

A turned ASIC board puts ±8 V onto ASIC clock and data pins and would likely destroy the bonded ASICs. Housing is set aside, but this one can kill the chips.

What to do
Add a key, or at least an orientation mark that stays visible after mating, and an orientation check in the bring-up steps.
Full entry
SYS-09 in the V3 list (evidence and history)
SYS-60Minor

All eight ASICs share the stimulation control lines, and their settings cannot be read back.

A chip that powers up with its stimulators enabled will stimulate whenever any chip in the stack does, and nothing can detect it.

What to do
Ask the ASIC designer what the stimulation registers hold after power-up and CHIP_RESET. Make it a firmware rule to load an all-off table into all eight chips after every power-up and reset, before STIM_EN goes high.

SYS-60 in the V3 list (evidence and history)
SYS-61Minor

There is no bring-up procedure, and a stacked head has no test points.

The first power-up of bonded ASICs depends on remembering scattered rules, and one missed step can destroy all eight ASICs of a head. Once stacked, the rails and signals cannot be probed.

What to do
Write a one-page order: HDMI board alone; Frontend alone; Frontend + FPGA-LDO without ASIC boards (check all nine rails); bare ASIC boards; bonded boards last, cable on last.
Includes
SYS-15 No board has a test pad on any of the nine ASIC rails, so rails must be probed on small capacitor pads., SYS-37 Once the head is stacked, FPGA configuration and the power-good/reset chain cannot be seen or probed., SYS-38 No board in the head has a test pad on any ASIC clock, sync, read, data or SPI line.

Measure on the first built stack, before building eight 4

SYS-01Major

The cable data rate is not set, and the only recorded rate (1.25 Gb/s) is too slow for eight ASICs.

Each cable's one GT lane carries all eight ASICs of a stack, and eight stacks need PCIe Gen3 x4 with packed samples at the PC.

What to do
Record the lane rate and framing (for example 2.5 Gb/s with packed data). Run an IBERT eye test over one real cable, and check Gen3 x4 training through the Thunderbolt adapter.
Includes
SYS-12 Eight stacks need PCIe Gen3 x4 to the host, but no record states Gen3 or packed samples as a requirement.

SYS-01 in the V3 list (evidence and history)
SYS-02Major

No cable is chosen, and a thin cable can push a stack into the 637 mA port limit.

A high-load stack on a 1.8 m cable with 30-32 AWG power wire reaches the limit and the Frontend browns out. JTAG TMS uses HDMI pin 2, which some cables leave out, and the plug must be under about 9.2 mm thick and 13 mm wide.

What to do
Write a cable spec (Micro-HDMI both ends, all 19 pins wired, 5 V loop under about 0.9 Ω, plug size) and measure a sample.
Includes
SYS-25 Each stack's power crosses one Micro-HDMI contact and one cable wire of unrecorded gauge, with little margin., SYS-26 A port in current limit leaves the Frontend browned out, not shut down, and the XEM cannot see the fault., SYS-33 No HDMI cable is specified, yet its plug must be under about 9.2 mm thick at the head and about 13 mm wide., SYS-20 Frontend JTAG uses HDMI pin 2 for TMS, a wire that cables without Ethernet may leave out.

SYS-03Major

Head heat is not estimated or limited.

About 1.45 W per stack gives a 19-34 K surface rise in still air, and the commercial-grade FPGA is rated only to 85 °C (DS181: https://docs.amd.com/v/u/en-US/ds181_Artix_7_Data_Sheet).

What to do
Set a head-surface temperature limit and measure one powered stack running the real bitstream.
Full entry
SYS-03 in the V3 list (evidence and history)
R2S-05Major

Each port's 44 ASIC outputs return through only two ground contacts.

Digital return current can flow into the analog ground and the reference, adding noise to every recording and disturbing the returned clocks. Pin map V6 now uses the spare contacts, so ground cannot be added to this revision.

What to do
Measure AGND-DGND noise and recording noise on the first stack. If it is too high, add ground contacts in the next ASIC/LDO revision.
Includes
SYS-23 Each J3/J4 joint has only four ground contacts, all near the middle, for about 60 signals., SYS-41 On the ASIC board the clock and strobe lanes switch between the AGND and DGND planes with no tie between them.

SYS-58Major

The enclosure has no room for the XEM8310's heat sink.

The 10-15 W module would run without a heat sink or fan in a closed box and may exceed its 70 °C rating.

What to do
Decide whether FANSINK-05 is fitted, measure its height, and cut the lid or add a fan opening. Then measure the box temperature with the real bitstream.
