SYS-02Major

High-load stacks reach the 637 mA port current limit on a 1.8 m cable with 30-32 AWG or thinner +5 V wire.

Each stack (Frontend, FPGA-LDO board and four ASIC boards) gets all its power through pin 19 of its Micro-HDMI cable, a single +5 V wire. The Frontend buck U16 draws close to constant power, so when the cable voltage drops its current rises. With a perfect cable the high-load case needs about 0.55-0.58 A, below the port switch's 637 mA minimum limit, but once the cable loop resistance passes about 0.9-1.3 ohm (30-32 AWG at 1.8 m) it reaches that limit, and at 34-36 AWG there is no usable operating point. The expected load works on every gauge; the Frontend power has never been estimated with Vivado/XPE and the chosen cable's wire gauge is unknown, so a power estimate and a measured cable loop resistance are needed.

SYS-10Minor

The FPGA cannot switch or monitor the ASIC rails or +/-12 V converter; they are on whenever the cable is live.

On the FPGA-LDO boards the shared enable for all nine ASIC regulators and the LT3582 +/-12 V converter is only a resistor and capacitor from VIN_3V3, and VIN_3V3 itself is always on once the cable is powered. No enable signal comes from the FPGA and no power-good (a regulator's 'output is in range' signal) goes back, so the FPGA cannot hold off, sequence, watch or shut down the ASIC supplies, and swapping an ASIC board means pulling the cable or hot-plugging. The back-powering at start-up claimed in the original does not happen: the ASIC rails rise about 15-22 ms after VIN_3V3, well after the FPGA's 1.5 V I/O bank (about 3 ms), while the FPGA pins are still undriven. Power-down order is not settled by the files and needs a scope capture; the open point is whether the ASIC designer's still-unknown sequencing and pin limits require FPGA control, which is cheap to add only before ordering.

Update 13 Nov: The ASIC designer said on 12 Nov 2026 that the power-up and power-down order does not matter and that signals on an unpowered chip do not matter, so the FPGA does not have to switch the rails. Pin map V6 carries no enable or power-good line, and on FPGA-LDO A layout V14 all rails still turn on from the 3.3 V input alone. What remains: the FPGA cannot switch off or see a failed rail, only one spare contact is left for a later change, and the rule to unplug the cable before an ASIC board is fitted or removed is not yet written down. Severity was major.
