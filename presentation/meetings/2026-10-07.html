03 / 13

Clock and boot decisions

The selected oscillator is 125 MHz LVDS at 1.8 V. M1 keeps the AC-coupling pair and uses a supplier-derived land pattern.

M1 omits the LMK splitter while the GTP ODIV2 fabric-clock path awaits Vivado verification.

The original slide asks for better than ±20 ppm but lists a ±25 ppm oscillator. That requirement still needs reconciliation.

The 1.8 V flash crosses the 1.5 V FPGA configuration interface through NVT4858. SPI x1 boot and brownout behavior need testing.

Sources: Zitong slide 1. M1 Zitong_Clock_Implementation.md. M1 Zitong_Flash_Implementation.md. Files and provenance

The oscillator fits the reference-clock interface as a prototype selection. Its quoted 186 fs jitter was characterized at 3.3 V, so it does not settle the 1.8 V clock qualification. We need to verify the internal fabric-clock route and resolve the ppm mismatch. NVT4858 comes from an SD/SDIO application, and the implemented SPI adaptation still requires physical boot checks. RMS phase jitter also does not verify the slide’s peak-to-peak cycle-to-cycle jitter requirement. The proposed LMK exact-part hyperlink differs from the typed suffix and needs checking if later fitted.

06 / 13

Simpler ASIC connections

M1 removes all 15 ASIC bus buffers and 56 parts overall. The component count falls from 343 to 287.

The original same-ASIC LDO and ECP5 reference circuits connect their digital signals directly.

M1 retains the outgoing 33 Ω damping arrays, passive defaults, boot holds and stimulation permission logic.

Removing buffers also removes their powered-off isolation. Controlled power sequencing and interface qualification remain necessary.

All 116 ASIC paths pass independent connectivity checks. Twenty-two receive-default arrays remain fitted.

Sources: M1 independent connectivity verification. 6 October same-ASIC LDO/ECP5 comparison. Files and provenance

The references support trying direct nominal 1.5 V signaling. Recording returns remain individual wires. Shared outgoing controls can have heavier loads as we scale to eight ASICs. We should add a targeted driver only where load or edge evidence supports it. We have not removed the 22 receive-default arrays or replaced them with a verified firmware policy. Native ERC, physical DRC and parity are zero, with PCB routing still open.
07 / 13

Gerald’s board-design guidance

Place repeated circuits clearly, following signal flow and short local power loops.

Match total clock/data timing within each related I/O group. Shorten detours before tuning.

Keep continuous ground references and deliberate return connections at layer changes. Place bypass capacitors at their ICs.

Use manufacturable FPGA escapes. Gerald’s 1–2, 1–3 and 1–4 blind-via spans need a fabricator-approved construction.

Keep necessary functions in a compact placement, with bottom mezzanine connectors and a clear overall schematic.

Sources: 6 October recorded Gerald feedback and routing plan. 4 October recorded routing directions. Files and provenance

The October 6 feedback highlighted routing quality, regular placement and unmatched delays. The earlier directions also call for power-distribution checks, practical blind-via escapes, compact placement and bottom connectors. Our engineering application is to review the full signal and return path, not only visual symmetry. A factory stackup and guaranteed ASIC timing still need evidence.

Next review decisions

Confirm the FPGA clock path, oscillator tolerance and SPI boot behavior for the selected parts.

Review the compact placement and carrier routing with Gerald, then route against an explicit timing and return-path plan.

Close ASIC pad limits, per-rail loads, power order and shared-control loading before operating the direct interface.

Check simultaneous connector mating, actual component bodies and the manufacturer’s stackup before fabrication.

Build the matched FPGA/host workflow and test one ASIC before assessing eight-chip operation.

Sources: M1 remaining operation gates. ASIC_REFERENCE U01–U09. Carrier manufacturing hold and routing plan. Files and provenance

Our next concrete review should use the native two-sheet FPGA drawing and the new carrier PCB. The requirements that still need ASIC-side evidence are pad levels, recording data-valid timing, rail currents and power-loss behavior. The fabrication stackup and mechanical seating need supplier or physical evidence. The one-chip XEM path is the first integration step, with eight-chip scaling following verified results.
