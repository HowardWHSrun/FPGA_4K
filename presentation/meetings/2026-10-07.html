Zitong's source calls XC7A35T-2CSG325I the final FPGA decision. Its requirements are XC7A series, at least two GTP lanes, more than 120 user I/Os, more than 100 units in stock, authorized AMD distributors and the smallest suitable package. The eight-chip statement describes the intended system, rather than demonstrated operation.
06 / 11

Zitong’s reference clock

AX3DCF1-125.0000T3: 125 MHz, 1.8 V LVDS oscillator.

100 nF external AC-coupling capacitors.

LMK1D2102LRGTR: split the clock to MGTREFCLK and MRCC.

Component proposal from Zitong’s original 7 October list.

Sources: Zitong_1007_Original.pptx, slide 1: Reference clock. Files and provenance

This slide preserves Zitong's proposed clock list and order. The source says Jiaao confirmed that one oscillator can serve the FPGA and GTP clock domains. The oscillator is described as 3.2 × 2.5 mm, ±25 ppm and 186 fs RMS phase jitter typical. The splitter is described as 1:2 LVDS, 1.8 V, up to 2 GHz and VQFN-16 at 3 × 3 mm. Review comments belong here: the source asks for better than ±20 ppm while listing ±25 ppm, and RMS phase jitter does not establish its <10 ps peak-to-peak cycle-to-cycle jitter request. The reported oscillator characterization uses 3.3 V rather than M1's selected 1.8 V. The typed LMK1D2102LRGTR suffix differs from the source's linked LMK1D2102RGTR. M1 adopts the oscillator and AC coupling, but provisionally omits the LMK while the GTP ODIV2 fabric-clock route awaits Vivado verification. Listing the LMK here records Zitong's proposal, rather than claiming it is fitted in M1.

Zitong’s flash and level translation

IS25WP128F-JKLE: 128 Mbit SPI NOR flash, 6 × 5 mm.

NVT4858HKZ: FPGA 1.5 V to flash 1.8 V translation.

Programming path: MCU → JTAG → FPGA → SPI → Flash.

Component proposal from Zitong’s original 7 October list.

Sources: Zitong_1007_Original.pptx, slide 1: Flash. Files and provenance

This slide preserves Zitong's flash section and programming workflow. Its requirements are small size, a single SPI interface, Vivado recognition and Artix-7 support. The source gives the ISSI flash a 1.65–1.95 V supply and up to 166 MHz operation. It gives the NXP translator 1.08–1.95 V A-side, 1.65–3.6 V B-side, up to 208 MHz and XQFN-16 at 2.6 × 1.8 mm. Reported inventory and future distributor arrival dates are dated source claims and stay out of the slide body. The source explicitly says NVT4858 was designed for SD/SDIO and that FPGA boot/configuration behavior still needs validation. M1 adopts these exact parts with SPI x1, adapted idle biases and separate boot holds. Physical boot and brownout behavior remain open. The displayed programming path is the source's intended workflow, rather than a completed software demonstration.

10 / 11

Gerald’s board-design guidance

Keep related clock and data paths short and matched.

Place bypass capacitors close to ICs and keep continuous ground returns.

Use compact, regular placement and manufacturer-approved FPGA escapes.

Next: clock and boot checks, carrier routing, and assembled connector fit.

Sources: 6 October recorded Gerald feedback and routing plan. 4 October recorded routing directions. Files and provenance

The October 6 feedback highlighted routing quality, regular placement and unmatched delays. The earlier directions also call for power-distribution checks, practical blind-via escapes, compact placement and bottom connectors. Our engineering application is to review the full signal and return path, not only visual symmetry. A factory stackup and guaranteed ASIC timing still need evidence. Our next concrete review should use the native two-sheet FPGA drawing and the new carrier PCB. The requirements that still need ASIC-side evidence are pad levels, recording data-valid timing, rail currents and power-loss behavior. The fabrication stackup and mechanical seating need supplier or physical evidence. The one-chip XEM path is the first integration step, with eight-chip scaling following verified results.
