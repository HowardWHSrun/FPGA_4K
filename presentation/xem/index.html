EXISTING LAB HARDWARE

XEM8310 seated on BRK8310.

Physical two-board stack

The purchased XEM8310 is an Artix UltraScale+ FPGA module used as the downstream receiver and controller; it is not a conventional MCU. BRK8310 is its separate breakout board. They currently mate directly through MC1, MC2 and MC3. The new three-port interposer would be inserted at that joint so three remote XC7A25T boards can stream to XEM at the same time.

CONNECTOR ROLES

Which board and connector carries what?

TOP · FPGA MODULE

XEM8310

Its underside MC3 is the high-speed vertical sandwich connector. It exposes twelve GTY channels, four in each of banks 226, 225 and 224. The proposed cables use three channels per bank for recording RX and the TX half of channel 0 for control.

Official contact list ↗
MIDDLE · PROPOSED

Interposer

Three Type-D receptacles would branch from XEM MC3 to three custom FPGA PCBs. Matching BRK high-speed contacts are left open to avoid Y stubs. The R7 logical schematic is checked; the R9 PCB routes six of twelve GTY pairs, but TX1 and control remain open on every cable.

BOTTOM · BREAKOUT

BRK8310

J1 Bulls Eye exposes bank 226 in the direct stack. J6 is the computer PCIe card edge using banks 224/225. Those lanes are assigned to two custom cables in three-link mode, so J6 PCIe cannot run concurrently.

PC PATH AND POWER

The initial host route is XEM USB.

Recording and control

The three remote FPGA boards use separate custom µHDMI cables and simultaneous XEM GTY links. XEM USB/FrontPanel is planned to deliver received samples to the PC. Link gateware, framing, buffering and sustained aggregate USB capture have not been measured.

See the three cable routes ↗

External 12 V source

The XEM does not generate a 12 V output for the remote boards. A shared external 12 V input would have to be divided into three separately protected cable branches, while also supplying the XEM. Total loads, contact/return ratings, fault isolation and inrush remain open.
